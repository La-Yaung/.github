## What LaYaung is

LaYaung is a learning app for Myanmar students sitting three of the hardest exams a Burmese student can take: the **Cambridge IGCSE**, the **American GED**, and the **Myanmar national curriculum for Grade 10 to 12**.

Most study material for these exams was never written with a Burmese student in mind. You're expected to learn a difficult subject and decode it in a second language at the same time. LaYaung removes that double barrier. Every lesson is explained in Burmese first, then teaches you the English terms you'll actually see on the exam paper. You read the idea in your own language, then meet it in English on your own terms.

And you don't sit and watch. Each lesson is something you do — tap, drag, type, answer — so the material sticks instead of washing over you. It runs on your phone, so a fifteen-minute wait for the bus becomes a real study session.

---

## The problem we started with

Talk to any Myanmar student aiming for an international qualification and you hear the same four complaints:

The good resources are English-only. Past papers, revision guides, the better YouTube channels — almost none of it exists in Burmese, so a lot of energy goes into translation instead of learning.

Nothing is tied to the real syllabus. You can study for months and still not know whether you've covered what Cambridge or the GED board will actually ask. Effort doesn't equal readiness.

There's no honest mirror. Without a way to test yourself against the real standard, "I think I'm ready" is just a feeling, usually proven wrong on exam day.

Feedback on written work is almost impossible to get. An essay or a long-form answer needs a human to read it and tell you why it lost marks. Online, that barely exists for Burmese students.

LaYaung was built to close all four gaps at once, in Burmese.

## How you actually study

Lessons are broken into small, active steps instead of one long video. Across the app there are nine kinds of practice, and each one trains a different skill:

**Checking what you know** — multiple-choice and true/false questions for fast self-tests at the end of a topic.

**Locking in vocabulary and terms** — flashcards for revision, and "mark the words" exercises where you pick out the right terms inside a passage. This is where the bilingual approach matters most: you learn the concept in Burmese and the keyword in English together.

**Building and arranging** — fill-in-the-blank, drag-the-words, ordering steps into the correct sequence, and matching pairs. These push you past recognising an answer into actually constructing it, which is what the exam demands.

**Working through maths** — a step-by-step maths input so you show your working, not just a final number.

Because the practice is varied, a study session doesn't feel like one long grind, and the different formats reinforce the same idea from several angles.

---

## Knowing if you're ready

Every lesson in LaYaung is linked to a real point in the official syllabus — a specific Cambridge IGCSE topic or a GED subject standard. That mapping does two things.

First, you can see your progress against the real exam, not against an arbitrary course. Second, when you take a check-up assessment, LaYaung doesn't just hand back a score. It shows you which syllabus areas are weak and sends you straight to the lessons that fix them. Instead of "revise everything again," you get "you're losing marks on [topic] — start here."

---

## Getting your writing checked

For essays and long-answer work, a number won't help you. You need a person.

Inside the app you can submit written work and have a teacher read it and respond directly — corrections, comments, and what to do differently next time. It's the part of having a real tutor that's hardest to get online, brought into the app for students who otherwise couldn't reach it.
